==================================================
THE TWO PROBLEMS MILESTONE 4 MUST SOLVE
==================================================

After personally watching Milestone 3, two major shortcomings are immediately obvious:

1. THE MONSTERS DO NOT MOVE AROUND THE ARENA ENOUGH.

They tend to meet, plant themselves near one location, and exchange blows.

The arena therefore feels like scenery rather than an active giant-monster battlefield.

2. THE MONSTERS ARE TOO SIMILAR.

They currently read as variations of essentially the same humanoid creature using largely the same combat language.

Pixel Monsters needs creatures with strongly different silhouettes, proportions, movement personalities, preferred ranges, and attack styles.

Milestone 4 should solve BOTH of these problems together.

Different anatomy should lead naturally to different movement and combat behavior.

==================================================
CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
==================================================

Pixel Monsters should begin developing a roster of strange, instantly recognizable giant fighters.

Use the design spirit of vintage collectible wrestling-monster toys such as M.U.S.C.L.E. as a STARTING POINT FOR DESIGN THINKING ONLY.

DO NOT:

- reproduce any specific copyrighted M.U.S.C.L.E. character
- copy recognizable individual toy designs
- use M.U.S.C.L.E. names, logos, costumes, or exact silhouettes

DO take inspiration from the broader philosophy:

- exaggerated anatomy
- bizarre proportions
- readable silhouettes
- wrestler-like physicality
- creatures whose shape immediately suggests how they fight
- strange humanoids that look memorable even in silhouette
- compact, sculptural character design
- fun physical archetypes taken completely seriously by the presentation

We want ORIGINAL Pixel Monsters.

==================================================
3. BLENDER DOES NOT REPLACE THE DESTRUCTIBLE BODY SYSTEM
==================================================

The actual gameplay creatures must remain compatible with Pixel Monsters' existing destructible cube architecture.

Each pristine monster still contains exactly:

1,000 VISIBLE DESTRUCTIBLE BODY CUBES.

Do not replace the cube creatures with conventional mesh characters.

A good pipeline may resemble:

Blender creature design
→ proportion / volume reference
→ conversion or procedural interpretation
→ exactly-1,000-cube gameplay body
→ Godot structural regions
→ animation / locomotion
→ combat
→ destruction

You may create tooling to assist this conversion.

If a voxelization or body-generation step is useful, build it robustly and document it.

The goal is for Blender to influence the creature's actual game silhouette, not just produce concept art that is ignored.

==================================================
4. EXACTLY 1,000 CUBES REMAINS A FUNDAMENTAL RULE
==================================================

Every intact monster should continue to use exactly 1,000 destructible body cubes.

This rule now becomes even more interesting because archetypes should DISTRIBUTE those cubes differently.

Examples:

a brute may spend more cubes on:

- shoulders
- chest
- fists
- arms

while spending fewer on:

- lower legs
- neck
- head

A kicking monster may invest heavily in:

- thighs
- calves
- pelvis
- feet

A headbutting creature may invest heavily in:

- skull
- neck
- shoulders

Do not secretly balance monsters by simply giving one more total body cubes.

Their strengths and vulnerabilities should emerge partly from WHERE the 1,000 cubes are invested.

Document the exact regional cube distribution for every archetype.

==================================================
5. BUILD AT LEAST THREE DISTINCT MONSTER ARCHETYPES
==================================================

Create at least THREE genuinely distinct monster archetypes.

They should not be simple recolors.

The following are design seeds, not mandatory exact implementations.

Use them as inspiration and improve them if you discover stronger designs.

--------------------------------------------------
ARCHETYPE A — THE BRUTE
--------------------------------------------------

Possible characteristics:

- huge shoulders
- enormous fists
- thick torso
- short neck
- relatively compact legs
- heavy forward posture

Combat identity:

- closes distance aggressively
- devastating hooks
- heavy straight punches
- overhead clubbing strike if appropriate
- shoves / body pressure
- poor reach compared with long-limbed monster
- reluctant to give ground

Movement:

- stalks forward
- corners opponent
- fewer lateral movements
- strongly prefers close range

Weakness:

- may be vulnerable to leg damage or long-range harassment

--------------------------------------------------
ARCHETYPE B — THE LONGARM
--------------------------------------------------

Possible characteristics:

- tall
- leaner torso
- disproportionately long arms
- narrower shoulders
- long reach
- unusual silhouette

Combat identity:

- long straight punches
- wide sweeping strikes
- long-range hooks
- possible two-handed hammer blow
- tries to hit before opponent reaches close range

Movement:

- gives ground
- circles more
- attempts to maintain reach
- backs away from brute pressure
- repositions frequently

Weakness:

- thinner arm connections
- less powerful at very close range

--------------------------------------------------
ARCHETYPE C — THE BULL / HEADBUTTER
--------------------------------------------------

Possible characteristics:

- enormous head
- thick neck
- massive upper torso
- comparatively smaller arms
- powerful shoulders
- forward-driving posture

Combat identity:

- headbutts
- shoulder checks
- short heavy punches
- body charges
- close-range collisions

Movement:

- may deliberately create distance
- then charge
- drives opponent backward
- does not rely heavily on arms

Important:

losing one or both arms should NOT cripple this monster nearly as much as it would cripple the Brute.

--------------------------------------------------

You may replace or modify these archetypes if Blender experimentation produces better original creatures.

The requirement is:

THREE MONSTERS THAT LOOK DIFFERENT,
MOVE DIFFERENTLY,
AND FIGHT DIFFERENTLY.

==================================================
6. BODY SHAPE MUST DRIVE COMBAT
==================================================

Do not simply give the same skeleton three visual skins and assign different damage numbers.

Monster anatomy should influence:

- reach
- attack selection
- preferred fighting distance
- locomotion
- attack animation
- balance
- structural vulnerabilities
- available attacks after limb loss
- AI strategy

Examples:

long arms
→ longer punch range

huge head / neck
→ powerful headbutts

massive shoulders
→ effective charges

large legs
→ stronger kicking game

small arms
→ less dependence on punching

heavy torso
→ harder to stagger but potentially top-heavy

This principle should be encoded cleanly so future monsters can be created without rewriting the combat engine.

BLENDER / BODY DESIGN

ATTACK ASSETS

==================================================
8. DIFFERENT ATTACK VOCABULARIES
==================================================

The monsters should no longer all fight from essentially the same move list.

There may be shared universal attacks, but each archetype needs signature attacks.

Examples:

BRUTE:
- crushing right hook
- left body shot
- heavy double-handed or overhead strike
- shove

LONGARM:
- long straight
- sweeping backhand
- wide hook
- hammer strike

HEADBUTTER:
- headbutt
- shoulder charge
- short hook
- driving body strike

Do not build twenty moves per creature.

Approximately 4–6 meaningful options per archetype is enough for now.

The purpose is differentiation.

==================================================
9. ARENA-WIDE MOVEMENT IS A PRIMARY GOAL
==================================================

The monsters currently remain too stationary.

Fix this deliberately.

A normal fight should MIGRATE through the environment.

The monsters should:

- advance
- retreat
- pursue
- circle
- reposition
- create distance
- close distance
- move after knockback
- chase a wounded opponent
- avoid remaining rooted to one small patch of arena

During a complete fight, it should be common for combat to move through multiple distinct portions of the arena.

==================================================
10. PREFERRED COMBAT RANGE
==================================================

Give different archetypes different desired ranges.

For example:

BRUTE:
close range

LONGARM:
medium / long melee range

HEADBUTTER:
alternates between separation and explosive close-range entry

This should naturally create movement.

Example sequence:

Brute advances.

Longarm backs away and circles.

Longarm lands a ranged punch.

Brute continues pursuing.

Longarm becomes cornered near buildings.

They exchange.

Longarm escapes sideways.

Fight moves down another street.

This is dramatically preferable to two monsters meeting in the middle and remaining there.

==================================================
11. RETREAT IS NOT COWARDICE
==================================================

Movement away from the opponent should be part of fighting.

AI may deliberately retreat to:

- restore preferred distance
- escape a bad close-range matchup
- create charge distance
- reposition after limb damage
- circle away from a damaged side
- avoid being trapped near arena boundary

Do not make every retreat long or passive.

The monsters should still strongly desire combat.

Movement should create better fighting, not long periods of avoidance.

==================================================
12. PURSUIT
==================================================

Aggressive monsters should actively pursue retreating opponents.

Do not allow:

Monster A backs away
→ Monster B stands still
→ fight stalls

The pursuer should:

- follow
- cut distance
- attempt to angle toward opponent
- resume attacking

Different archetypes may pursue differently.

==================================================
13. CIRCLING
==================================================

Add meaningful lateral movement.

Do not create constant FPS-style strafing.

Instead use occasional broad repositioning arcs.

Monsters might circle to:

- improve facing
- avoid a damaged leg
- maintain preferred distance
- escape arena boundary
- seek attack angle
- avoid a charge

Movement should remain slow and enormous.

==================================================
14. KNOCKBACK SHOULD HELP MOVE THE FIGHT
==================================================

Large impacts should sometimes displace opponents enough to shift the battle location.

Do not turn monsters into lightweight projectiles.

But a devastating hit may:

- drive opponent several steps backward
- force recovery movement
- cause stagger retreat
- redirect the engagement

The aggressor should then decide whether to pursue.

This can make heavy impacts alter the geography of the fight.

==================================================
15. CHARGE ATTACKS
==================================================

At least one archetype should demonstrate an attack that actively uses arena space.

The headbutter/bull archetype is an ideal candidate.

Possible sequence:

AI decides it wants charge distance.

→ backs away several meters

→ turns/faces opponent

→ brief preparation

→ accelerates into a charge

→ opponent may reposition or brace

→ collision produces major impact

This should feel deliberate.

Do not trigger charges at point-blank range.

==================================================
16. DAMAGE SHOULD ALTER MOVEMENT STRATEGY
==================================================

Existing structural damage systems must interact with the new movement model.

Examples:

damaged right leg
→ reduced turn strength toward one side

severely damaged leg
→ slower pursuit

missing leg
→ major mobility compromise

lost arm
→ preferred fighting distance may change

Longarm loses one long arm
→ may become much less effective at range and be forced into another behavior

Headbutter loses both arms
→ remains dangerous because its primary anatomy is still useful

Do not treat structural damage merely as disabled attack buttons.

It should change the fight spatially.

==================================================
17. COMBATANTS SHOULD NOTICE ARENA BOUNDARIES
==================================================

Prevent fights from degenerating at arena edges.

Monsters near unusable boundaries should attempt to rotate or move back toward viable space.

Do not use invisible-force-field behavior that looks artificial if avoidable.

AI should prefer repositioning naturally.

If necessary, use invisible arena guidance internally, but make behavior visually plausible.

==================================================
18. SIMPLE NAVIGATION, NOT A CHESS ENGINE
==================================================

Do not overcomplicate navigation.

We do NOT need advanced tactical pathfinding through an entire city yet.

The environment remains mostly open.

Use simple robust navigation appropriate for enormous creatures.

Avoid monsters getting caught on:

- small props
- curbs
- decorative geometry
- streetlights
- minor scenery

Large monsters should effectively ignore or step through small-scale navigation obstacles where appropriate.

==================================================
19. SHOW THE BATTLE'S PATH
==================================================

One of the visual rewards of arena movement should be that the battlefield tells the story of where the monsters fought.

Existing detached cubes should naturally help.

By late fight, the observer may see:

- cube debris near opening exchange
- another debris field farther down the street
- damaged monster pieces near another location
- final wreckage somewhere else

Do not artificially paint a trail.

Let the existing destruction system create one.

==================================================
20. DIRECTOR CAMERA MUST ADAPT TO MOVING FIGHTS
==================================================

The cinematic camera built in Milestone 3 must now cope with fights that travel significantly.

Improve it where necessary.

The director should:

- reacquire moving combatants
- anticipate pursuit
- reframe charges
- reposition when fight enters another area
- avoid remaining behind at the old fight location
- maintain useful composition during retreats
- capture wide shots when monsters separate
- tighten framing when they re-engage

Arena movement should make the director BETTER, not break it.

==================================================
21. ENVIRONMENT SHOULD SUPPORT MOVEMENT
==================================================

Inspect the current city layout.

Adjust it if necessary to create useful fighting spaces.

We want:

- broad streets
- intersections
- open plazas or industrial areas
- routes between districts
- recognizable landmarks

Do not create narrow alleys where giant monsters constantly snag.

The arena should support migration.

Distinct zones are desirable.

For example:

- central intersection
- warehouse district
- plaza
- waterfront/industrial edge
- broad avenue

Even simple geometry can create a sense of journey.

Once the three archetypes are working, autonomous fights should no longer always use the same pair.

At launch, select two combatants from the available roster.

Examples:

Longarm vs Headbutter

Headbutter vs Brute

==================================================
23. MONSTER VISUAL IDENTITY
==================================================

Color remains useful, but silhouette must carry identity.

If all materials were temporarily gray, I should still be able to recognize which archetype I am looking at.

Test this.

Use Blender silhouette views or renders if useful.

Do not rely solely on:

red monster
blue monster
green monster

Color supports identity.

Shape creates identity.

==================================================
24. KEEP MATERIALS SIMPLE ENOUGH FOR DESTRUCTION
==================================================

Do not create elaborate material complexity that makes the individual cubes difficult to read.

Cube structure must remain visually obvious.

Damage cavities must remain readable.

Consider modest variation such as:

- slightly different roughness
- subtle body-region variation
- restrained accent colors
- different base palettes

but do not make them visually noisy.

==================================================

For each new archetype:

1. Create initial creature study in Blender.

2. Inspect front, side, three-quarter, and silhouette.

3. Export/reference the proportions into the Godot 1,000-cube body system.

4. Run the creature in Godot.

5. Observe it moving and fighting.

6. Identify visual or animation weaknesses.

7. Return to Blender where useful.

8. Revise.

9. Reintegrate.

10. Repeat.

Do not treat Blender as a one-way asset factory.

Use the live two-application workflow we intentionally configured.

==================================================
29. WATCH FOR MOVEMENT FAILURE MODES
==================================================

Specifically look for:

- foot sliding
- moonwalking
- constant tiny rotations
- twitching between movement decisions
- monsters orbiting forever
- backpedaling forever
- monsters walking through each other
- monsters constantly missing because both are moving
- attacks locking monsters in place too long
- fights always returning to exact center
- movement looking human-sized rather than gigantic
- sudden instantaneous facing changes

Fix these through actual observation.

==================================================
30. KEEP MOVEMENT MASSIVE
==================================================

More movement does NOT mean faster movement.

This is critical.

We want:

MORE GEOGRAPHICAL TRAVEL

not:

FASTER ARCADE CHARACTERS.

The monsters should still feel colossal.

They should cover more of the arena because their tactical behavior causes them to walk, pursue, retreat, circle, and reposition over the duration of the fight.

Preserve:

- inertia
- acceleration
- deliberate turning
- committed footsteps
- heavy stopping
- physical follow-through

==================================================
THE DEFINING TEST
==================================================

Milestone 4 succeeds if I can launch Pixel Monsters several times and immediately feel that I am watching DIFFERENT giant-monster movies.

One fight might be:

a squat massive brute relentlessly stalking a long-armed creature through city streets while the long-armed monster gives ground and lashes out from range.

Another might be:

a giant-headed creature deliberately backing away across an intersection, lowering itself, then charging forward and smashing its skull into the opponent.

Another might become:

a crippled monster limping away on a ruined leg while its opponent pursues it into another part of the arena.

The monsters should no longer feel like:

RED GUY versus BLUE GUY.

They should feel like distinct creatures whose bodies determine how they fight.

And the battle should no longer belong to one patch of pavement.

It should travel.
